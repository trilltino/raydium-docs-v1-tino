## One-paragraph summary

CPMM — **C**onstant **P**roduct **M**arket **M**aker, officially the "Standard AMM" in Raydium's UI — is a native Solana implementation of the classic `x · y = k` AMM. No OpenBook order book, no Serum legacy, no external dependencies beyond SPL Token and Token-2022. Every pool is a triple of (two token vaults, one LP mint) governed by a program-owned authority PDA, priced by the product of the vault balances, less the fees still owed to the protocol, the fund and the pool creator. It is the AMM Raydium recommends for new constant-product pools.

## What CPMM gives you

<Info>
  **Creator-fee collection update.** Any payer can trigger `CollectCreatorFeePermissionless`. Funds still go only to the pool creator's canonical token accounts, and the existing creator-signed `CollectCreatorFee` remains available. Since 2026-09-19 both paths hand a configurable share of the accrued creator fee to the protocol on the way out, and both take extra accounts appended at the end of their lists — one on `CollectCreatorFee`, two on `CollectCreatorFeePermissionless`. See [Instructions](/docs/en/products/cpmm/instructions#collectcreatorfeepermissionless) and [Fees](/docs/en/products/cpmm/fees#protocol-share-of-the-creator-fee).

  **Creator fee open to every pool creator (2026-10-30).** The new `InitializeV2` lets any creator enable the creator fee and choose its side at creation, without a permission grant. `Initialize` is unchanged. See [Instructions](/docs/en/products/cpmm/instructions#initializev2).
</Info>

- **Token-2022 with a vetted extension allow-list.** CPMM does **not** accept arbitrary Token-2022 mints. The program enforces a whitelist of safe extensions at pool creation: `TransferFeeConfig`, `MetadataPointer`, `TokenMetadata`, `InterestBearingConfig`, and `ScaledUiAmount`. Any other extension on the mint makes pool creation fail with `NotSupportMint`, unless an admin has registered the mint through its `SupportMintAssociated` PDA and the creator passes that PDA in the creation instruction's remaining accounts. That registry is the only bypass since the 2026-09 upgrade removed the hardcoded mint whitelist. Transfer fees in particular affect swap math and are applied on the correct side of the trade — see [`algorithms/token-2022-transfer-fees`](/docs/en/algorithms/token-2022-transfer-fees).
- **Predictable fees.** Each pool references an `AmmConfig` selected at creation. The config carries a trade-fee rate (split between LPs, protocol, and fund) and a separate, independent creator-fee rate. The creator fee is its own bucket — never a slice of the trade fee. Pools opt into charging it at creation, through `InitializeV2` or the permissioned `InitializeWithPermission`. The protocol can retain a share **of the creator bucket**, set per fee tier or per creator and applied when the creator fee is collected, which leaves swap pricing untouched. On 2026-11-21, fee tiers 0–20 defaulted to a 5% protocol share and tiers 21–24 defaulted to none; per-creator overrides can replace those defaults. Defaults and the full split math are in [`products/cpmm/fees`](/docs/en/products/cpmm/fees).
- **On-chain TWAP via an observation ring buffer.** Every swap updates an `observation` account. External contracts can read a cumulative-price observation to compute a TWAP without a custom oracle. Entries are at least 15 seconds apart; see [Math](/docs/en/products/cpmm/math#observation-update-rule).
- **Flat account layout.** A pool is fully described by six addresses (authority, pool state, LP mint, two vaults, observation). No per-market OpenBook account, no event queue, no request queue. A swap still passes 13 accounts against 8 for an AMM v4 V2 swap, and it uses more compute; see the table below.
- **Burn-and-earn compatible.** LP tokens can be locked under the [LP Lock](/docs/en/reference/program-addresses) program. The holder of the resulting Fee Key can keep claiming the locked position's LP fees but can't withdraw the liquidity. Used for "permanent" liquidity launches. This is separate from the CPMM creator fee: collection rights remain with `pool_creator`, less any protocol share applied at collection.

## What CPMM is not

- **Not concentrated.** Liquidity is spread evenly across the whole price range, like Uniswap v2. If you need capital-efficient market making — i.e., concentrating liquidity near the current price — use [CLMM](/docs/en/products/clmm).
- **Not hybrid.** CPMM pools never placed resting orders on an order book; [AMM v4](/docs/en/products/amm-v4) did until its July 2026 upgrade. Multi-hop trades reach CPMM pools through Raydium's [AMM Routing](/docs/en/reference/program-addresses) program and third-party aggregators, not through a CLOB.
- **Not launchable for arbitrary curves.** The curve is hard-coded to constant product. If you want a bonding curve for a token launch, use [LaunchLab](/docs/en/products/launchlab). Its authorized migration moves a completed launch into a CPMM pool.

## How CPMM differs from AMM v4

| Dimension | AMM v4 | CPMM |
|-----------|--------|------|
| Curve | Constant product | Constant product |
| OpenBook dependency | Inert (originally placed orders on an OpenBook market; integration deactivated) | No |
| Token-2022 support | No (SPL Token only) | **Yes** (including transfer fees) |
| Accounts per swap | 8 (`SwapBaseInV2`, `SwapBaseOutV2`) | 13 (`SwapBaseInput`, `SwapBaseOutput`) |
| Typical compute per swap | ≈15,400 CU (`SwapBaseInV2`, 98 swaps) | ≈25,200 CU (`SwapBaseInput`, 384 swaps) |
| TWAP oracle | No native oracle account | `observation` ring buffer |
| New pool creation today | Not the default (program still accepts it) | Default |
| Status | Fully operational | Active, recommended |

The compute figures are medians from a mainnet sample taken on 2026-11-20. They count only the pool program's own compute, including its token transfers, not the whole transaction. In the same sample, 9 CPMM `SwapBaseOutput` calls had a median of 24,935 CU. Simulate your own transaction before you set a compute limit.

A deeper treatment of the migration story is in [`protocol-overview/versions-and-migration`](/docs/en/protocol-overview/versions-and-migration).

## Mental model

A CPMM pool is a program-owned object over three balances: `vault0` (token0), `vault1` (token1), and the pool's LP supply. `PoolState.lp_supply` tracks depositors' claim on the pool; the token vaults hold the actual assets. `lp_supply` is never below the LP mint's supply, because 100 units are locked at creation and LP burned outside the program stays counted. Everything else — the authority PDA, the observation account, the fee-config pointer — is bookkeeping to make that three-variable relationship tradable, fee-collecting, and observable.

Every user-facing operation collapses to a well-defined state transition:

- **Deposit:** transfer token0 and token1 in, mint LP to user, no change to price.
- **Withdraw:** burn LP from user, transfer token0 and token1 out in the pool's current ratio, no change to price.
- **Swap:** transfer one token in, transfer the other out; the ratio moves along the `x · y = k` curve (minus fees); the observation account accumulates the price that held before the swap.

Fee collection is a separate transaction; it does not happen on every swap. Protocol and fund collection require their respective owner key or the program admin. Creator fees can be collected either by the creator-signed path or by a permissionless caller through a destination-constrained path; either way the protocol's configured share of the accrued creator fee is moved into the pool's protocol bucket rather than paid out. The math is spelled out in [`products/cpmm/math`](/docs/en/products/cpmm/math) and the instruction set in [`products/cpmm/instructions`](/docs/en/products/cpmm/instructions).

## When to choose CPMM

Pick CPMM when:

- You are launching a new token or a new pair and do not have strong opinions about which range will see trading.
- One or both of the tokens uses Token-2022 extensions.
- You want a simple fee-per-trade model over a dynamic, tick-based one.
- You are integrating and want to route through Raydium without taking on the complexity of CLMM positions.

Prefer [CLMM](/docs/en/products/clmm) when:

- The pair is stable or highly correlated (stablecoin-stablecoin, LST-SOL) and you want to concentrate liquidity around parity.
- You are a market-making team willing to actively manage ranges for higher fee APR per dollar of TVL.

Prefer [AMM v4](/docs/en/products/amm-v4) when:

- You are migrating existing AMM v4 tooling and are not creating a new pool.

(Note: AMM v4's OpenBook hybrid mode is no longer active — that's not a reason to choose AMM v4 anymore.)
